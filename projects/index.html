Selected projects involving soil and environmental sensing, agricultural water management, machine learning, data analysis, and commercial applications of environmental monitoring technology.

2022

Cannabis Fertigation Based on Sensor Data

Cannabis Science Conference • Long Beach, California

Project Description & Application

Cannabis cultivation is an emerging agricultural science where precise water management can affect both plant performance and crop value. This project investigated sensor-based fertigation and controlled water stress during the final growth stage before harvest. The work also evaluated water distribution in small growing containers, with potential applications to flowers, ornamental plants, and other greenhouse crops.

Commercial Relevance

Application of monitoring systems to improve crop management, yield, and crop value.

2014

Improved Water Supply Forecasts Using Soil Moisture Data

4th International Symposium on Soil Water Measurement • Montreal, Quebec

Project Description & Application

This pilot study investigated whether soil moisture and soil-property data could improve streamflow forecasting. A machine-learning model was developed to combine soil moisture measurements with existing forecasting information, with potential applications to water-resource management and the hydroelectric power market.

Commercial Relevance

Application of soil sensors and monitoring stations to improve river-flow forecasts for hydropower applications.

2021–2022

Soil Water Drainage Assessment Using Fast Fourier Transform

Midwest U.S. stakeholders

Project Description & Application

Developed an algorithm using Fast Fourier Transform analysis of time-series soil moisture data to identify drainage and over-irrigation at multiple soil depths. The approach was investigated for managing agricultural runoff by optimizing the application of nutrient-rich water to row crops.

Commercial Relevance

Application of soil monitoring stations to fertigation systems and agricultural water-management programs.

2023–2024

Irrigation Management Using Machine Learning

European Geosciences Union Annual Meeting • Vienna, Austria

Project Description & Application

Developed and tested a machine-learning approach for irrigation management using aggregated soil moisture measurements from a single blueberry growing season combined with publicly available evapotranspiration data. The model predicts when crop water stress is likely to occur, providing irrigators with an indication of when irrigation may be required.

Commercial Relevance

Potential to reduce field sensor requirements while providing irrigation-management information through monitoring stations and cloud services.

2023

Machine Learning Method for Determining Soil Salinity Using TDR

Stakeholder presentations

Project Description & Application

Developed a machine-learning method for estimating soil salinity from electrical signals generated by Time Domain Reflectometry (TDR) sensors. Using Python, key features of the TDR signal were identified and related to soil properties, then used with training data to develop machine-learning regression models.

Commercial Relevance

Expands the potential application of TDR sensors beyond soil-moisture measurement to soil-salinity assessment.

2025

Deployment of Landslide Risk Detection Systems in Southeast Asia

Stakeholder presentations

Project Description & Application

Investigated the use of real-time soil hydraulic monitoring to support landslide risk detection in Southeast Asia. The approach focuses on monitoring soil hydraulic head, a dynamic parameter related to soil water content and water-table conditions, alongside static soil-stability characteristics.

Commercial Relevance

Application of monitoring stations, soil sensors, and cloud services to landslide monitoring and government safety programs.

Irrigation Optimization for Greenhouse-Grown Evergreen Ornamentals

CANVAS • Portland, Oregon

Project Description & Application

2025–2026

Commercial Water Retention Curve Laboratory

EuroSoil • Seville, Spain • EGU • Other stakeholders

Project Description & Application

Developed and piloted a commercial laboratory approach for measuring soil water retention curves using real-time monitoring during laboratory testing. Incorporating sensors directly into soil samples during testing has the potential to accelerate measurements and reduce errors while making soil-specific water retention data more practical for commercial applications.

Commercial Relevance

Commercial applications include soil sensors, soil-water retention laboratory equipment, and testing services for governmental water-resource authorities.
